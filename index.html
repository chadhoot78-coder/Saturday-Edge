How the number is built

Team rating. Every FBS game played this season counts: point margin (home field removed, capped at ±35 so a 70-point rout counts like a 35-point one), yards-per-play margin, and turnover margin, adjusted for who the opponent was. Ratings are shrunk toward average when the sample is small — 70% after one game, about 40% after four, 20% after ten — so the stats earn trust as the season goes.

The prior. A rating has to start somewhere before a team has played. Prior: Market (the default since v4.1) starts every team at the rating the betting lines imply — solved across all the games on the board, the same way the results model adjusts for opponents — so with no evidence the model line simply is the market line, and the stats only move it where they disagree. Prior: Average is the original behaviour: every team starts at zero, which early in the season makes the model line shorter than the spread in almost every game and leans the picks toward the underdog. The button flips between them instantly, the backtest re-runs, and past grades stay as they were recorded.

FCS opponents. Teams outside the FBS are anchored to a −22 rating (roughly where the market puts an average FCS visitor) and only drift from it with heavy shrinkage. Games against them still feed the FBS team's rating, but they're left out of the backtest — and, since v3, out of the picks too. A −22 prior against a 55-point line produced "85% to cover" underdogs that lost by 80, so a game with an FCS team shows the market number only: no ATS side, no total play, no stake. The bar and win % on those cards are the market's, not the model's.

Neutral sites & rest. Kickoff classics, rivalry games at neutral venues, and conference title games get no home-field points. The model line gets ±0.3 points per day of rest advantage (byes, Friday games). ESPN injury data is thin for college football, so injuries are applied only when the feed has them.

Weather. For outdoor games in the next two weeks, the forecast at the venue for kickoff hour comes from Open-Meteo (free, no key). Wind above 12 mph trims the projected total by a quarter point per mph (up to 6), a likely rain trims it 2 more, and a hard freeze 1.5. It never touches the spread — the market already prices weather into the side better than a hobby model can. Domes are skipped.

Bet log & Kelly. Log a play to track it. Stakes suggest half-Kelly from the model's probability and the price against your bankroll — half because the model's probabilities are noisier than it thinks — and are capped at 5% of the bankroll no matter what the number says, because a model that's wrong about a game is usually most wrong when it's most confident. Closing-line value is recorded when the game kicks off. Until then the log shows how far the line has moved from your number; once the game is on it shows whether the bet is covering and by how much, live.

Totals. Each team's points scored and allowed so far, adjusted for the opponents they faced and shrunk toward the FBS average of 28.5 per team on the same trust curve as the ratings, give a projected total. It blends with the market total at the same weight as the spread, and the over/under probability comes from the same 16-point curve. Break-even at -110 is 52.4%, so a value tag on a total means the model clears that by 3+ points.

Past and future weeks. The ◀ ▶ arrows step through the season. A past week is re-scored with only the ratings that existed before it kicked off (the same walk-forward rule as the backtest), so what you see is what the model would have said that Saturday, not hindsight. Future weeks show whatever lines the books have posted.

Alerts. Turn on Alerts and the app sends a notification when a line moves half a point or more on a play or a logged bet. It checks while the app is open or in a background tab; this is a one-file app with no server, so it can't wake a closed phone.

Line shopping. With a free key from the-odds-api.com pasted in, every upcoming game shows the best available number for the pick's side across the US books (and DraftKings' number on hover), and Log records that book, line and price instead of the consensus at -110. Getting +3.5 where the consensus says +3 is worth more over a season than any model tweak. The model itself still reads ESPN's consensus line.

Learning. After each completed week the app replays the season: for every past week it rebuilds ratings from only the weeks before, predicts that week, and scores each stats/market weight and shrink speed against the real margins. The Auto button uses whatever backtested best. With 60-odd FBS games a week it has something to say after Week 3.

Line tracking. The first spread the app sees for a game is saved as the opener. ▲ means the line has moved toward the pick (the market agrees), ▼ means it's moved away. Graded picks are saved so the record carries week to week.

Model line. Home rating − away rating + 2.5 pts home field (0 at a neutral site).

Blend. The slider mixes the model line with the market line. Default is 35% stats / 65% market, because the line already prices injuries, weather, and matchups. Nudge it toward stats as the season goes on.

Probability. Expected margin ÷ 16 (the historical std. dev. of FBS margins, wider than the NFL's 13.5) through a normal curve. ATS pick. Every game gets a side: whichever team the blended margin favors against the number, with the chance it covers and the dollar EV at -110. Margins are modeled on a discrete curve with a little extra weight on 3 and 7 — key numbers matter in college, but less than in the pros. College football has no ties, so a 0 margin is impossible. A pick is marked a play only when the Lab (below) shows that size of disagreement with the line has covered 53%+ over 100+ games in 2023–25; otherwise it's a lean, shown with its Lab rate. Once a game is final the pick grades W/L and the record at the top updates.

Coverage. Regular season, FBS games only (Weeks 1–16 on ESPN's calendar, including conference championships). Bowls and the playoff aren't loaded.
