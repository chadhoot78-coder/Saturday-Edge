How the number is built

Team rating. Every FBS game played this season counts: point margin (home field removed, capped at ±35 so a 70-point rout counts like a 35-point one), yards-per-play margin, and turnover margin, adjusted for who the opponent was. Ratings are shrunk toward average when the sample is small — 70% after one game, about 40% after four, 20% after ten — so the stats earn trust as the season goes.

FCS opponents. Teams outside the FBS are anchored to a −22 rating (roughly where the market puts an average FCS visitor) and only drift from it with heavy shrinkage. Games against them still feed the FBS team's rating, but they're left out of the backtest, and a line on one is a rough prior at best.

Neutral sites & rest. Kickoff classics, rivalry games at neutral venues, and conference title games get no home-field points. The model line gets ±0.3 points per day of rest advantage (byes, Friday games). ESPN injury data is thin for college football, so injuries are applied only when the feed has them.

Bet log & Kelly. Log a play to track it. Stakes suggest half-Kelly from the model's probability and the price against your bankroll — half because the model's probabilities are noisier than it thinks. Closing-line value is recorded when the game kicks off.

Totals. Each team's points scored and allowed so far, adjusted for the opponents they faced and shrunk toward the FBS average of 28.5 per team on the same trust curve as the ratings, give a projected total. It blends with the market total at the same weight as the spread, and the over/under probability comes from the same 16-point curve. Break-even at -110 is 52.4%, so a value tag on a total means the model clears that by 3+ points.

Learning. After each completed week the app replays the season: for every past week it rebuilds ratings from only the weeks before, predicts that week, and scores each stats/market weight and shrink speed against the real margins. The Auto button uses whatever backtested best. With 60-odd FBS games a week it has something to say after Week 3.

Line tracking. The first spread the app sees for a game is saved as the opener. ▲ means the line has moved toward the pick (the market agrees), ▼ means it's moved away. Graded picks are saved so the record carries week to week.

Model line. Home rating − away rating + 2.5 pts home field (0 at a neutral site).

Blend. The slider mixes the model line with the market line. Default is 35% stats / 65% market, because the line already prices injuries, weather, and matchups. Nudge it toward stats as the season goes on.

Probability. Expected margin ÷ 16 (the historical std. dev. of FBS margins, wider than the NFL's 13.5) through a normal curve. ATS pick. Every game gets a side: whichever team the blended margin favors against the number, with the chance it covers and the dollar EV at -110. Margins are modeled on a discrete curve with a little extra weight on 3 and 7 — key numbers matter in college, but less than in the pros. College football has no ties, so a 0 margin is impossible. A pick is a play only when the cover chance is ≥54.5% (break-even is 52.4%); otherwise it's a pass. Once a game is final the pick grades W/L and the record at the top updates.

Coverage. Regular season, FBS games only (Weeks 1–16 on ESPN's calendar, including conference championships). Bowls and the playoff aren't loaded.
